Small-sample exploratory study

Weak-model debate
needs a stable verifier.

Giving one agent a dedicated checking role makes correction more directional. But having a checker does not make the answer reliable: the checker can still be swayed by its peers.

Model: Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K: 40 questions / condition SVAMP: 20 questions / condition Plain-text reasoning · no tools

Finding 01

Roles can change correction direction

The checker condition rises from first to final turn on both GSM8K and SVAMP. The homogeneous condition does not rise consistently.

A verification role
is not verification ability.

The checker appears to change what the group attends to and preserves, rather than becoming an automatic judge of truth.

Finding 02

More agents do not yield monotonic gains

After two turns, 3 agents peak at 75%. Five to seven agents plateau at 70%, while mean latency grows from 4.0 to 45.3 seconds.

Finding 03

Direction is not a guarantee

Replacing one solver with a checker gives a suggestive signal. Simply adding a fourth checker does not improve final accuracy.

Failure cases

How wrong answers become persuasive

A correct candidate existed in the first turn. The failure was deciding which explanation to trust, not the absence of a correct solution.

test-888 · cookies

A typo becomes the majority's fact

Two initially correct agents copy a more elaborate but wrong explanation.

10→6→6→6

The model reads “5 five cookies” as 5×5. The explanation becomes longer and more reasoning-like, but not more correct.

test-192 · trees

A correct step, then a flipped conclusion

The last turn correctly calculates 39 dead trees, then reports 39 as the number remaining.

91→515→91→39

Debate adds steps, but does not reliably verify what quantity the final number actually answers.

Claim boundary

What does this study show?

Supported by these results

  • A dedicated checking role can steer correction in weak-model debate.
  • More turns, longer explanations, or more agents are not reliable verification mechanisms by themselves.
  • The checker should be isolated from circular peer influence.

Not supported by these results

  • “A checker always improves final accuracy.”
  • “More agents produce better answers.”
  • A universal effect size or optimal agent count from independent runs with n=20–40.
Next test

Freeze the same solver outputs, then ask an independent adjudicator to make one final decision. This separates verification ability from contamination by repeated peer feedback.
